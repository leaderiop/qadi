### Gate 14 probes: do they test the changed shape?

All of these are in the consumer in `scripts/check-package-install.mjs`:

- **Type probes.** `TYPE_ONLY_PROBES` gains `PortDescription`, `PortFixtureData` and `PortSpanName` (lines 114-121).
  - `attributeDescription` has the full 11-argument `PortDescription`, including `PortSpanOf<"AttributeResolver">` and `PortFixtureData["AttributeResolver"]`. It is built from the packed `PORTS.AttributeResolver` with its own `fixture`.
  - `fixtureData` is a `PortFixtureData` holding all five ports.
  - `attributeSpan` is a `PortSpanName` and must equal `"qadi.attribute"`.
- **Runtime probes.** `RUNTIME_PROBES = ["PORT_NAMES", "tabulatePorts"]` (line 132). The module hook wraps both in the packed `@qadi/core` and counts a call, or a property read, made by the run itself.
  - The run checks that `PORT_NAMES` is in registry order and that each port's span is distinct.
  - It builds every port through `tabulatePorts((d) => d.fixture)`, checks the builders equal `PORT_FIXTURES`, and evaluates through them.
  - Evaluating through the description's own `fixture` gives `Deny`, and through the overload's attribute builder gives `Allow`.
- **The refusal probe (`fixture: undefined` under `@ts-expect-error`).** `REFUSED_SHAPE_PROBES` gains `["PortDescription", "fixture"]` (line 154). I judge this form honest:
  - The consumer's tsconfig is `strict` without `exactOptionalPropertyTypes` (line ~1036).
  - If `fixture` became optional, or widened to admit `undefined`, the literal would compile. The directive would then go unused, which is TS2578, and the gate would fail.
  - If `fixture` were removed from the type, the refusal literal would still error (an excess property). But the positive twin `attributeDescription = { ...attributeFacts, fixture: … }`, the destructure of `PORTS.AttributeResolver.fixture` and the call `attributeDescription.fixture(...)` would all fail. The fixture-dropped block shows the positive twin failing with TS2741.
  - If the directive were deleted, the literal would fail with TS2322 (refused-directive-deleted). If the literal were deleted, the guard fails (refused-literal-deleted).
  - So required, present and not `undefined` are all held between the refusal and its twin.
  - The one thing the refusal alone does not prove is that a literal *omitting* `fixture` is refused. The fixer explains why: TS2741 lands on the declaration, where the guard cannot read a directive. The positive twin's required member, and the fixture-dropped evidence, cover that case instead.
  - The comment calls the refused literal "the pre-0.13 shape". Strictly it is the shape with `fixture` stated `undefined`. That is a naming nit, not a gap.
