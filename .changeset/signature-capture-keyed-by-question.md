A captured signature now answers the question it was captured for. Before, `signatureObligationHandler` gave `capture` no resource, so a flow that stored what it captured stored it subject-wide: a default-scope `hasSignature` could not find it and `scope: "Any"` found it for every resource. **Breaking:** `SignatureCaptureRequest` gains required `evaluationId`, `resourceId`, `action` and `resource` (code that constructs a request must supply them; `capture` implementations just receive more), and `signatureObligationHandler` takes an `ObligationDischarge` and no longer requires `CurrentSubject`: the signer is the subject the allow was decided for. A `capture` that returns a signature for a different signer or meaning than asked now fails the discharge (`SignatureCaptureError`). New `SignatureLedger`, `signatureHistoryFromLedger`, `ledgerEntryOf` and `SignatureLedgerTest`: pass `{ ledger }` to the handler and the signature `capture` returned is recorded under `(signer, resourceId)`, read back as `SignatureHistory`; a ledger that cannot record fails the discharge. Without a ledger, `capture` must persist where your `SignatureHistory` reads, keyed by the request.
